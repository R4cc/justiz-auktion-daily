# Cohesive trader loop

Daily is appraisal practice that earns trading capital and one sealed reward. Its three choices claim and open, keep, or list the same saved reward. Inventory is the place to keep, auction or stock a find, with state, category and rarity filters. Cases contain one mystery item; palettes contain three themed finds. Store ownership is optional: a full town or the three-store cap never blocks player auctions.

The five primary destinations are Daily, Auction House, Inventory, Your Stores and Town. Auction House combines Browse, My bids (including outcomes and winning reveals), and My listings. Filters distinguish individual items, stock bundles and mystery lots. `/marketplace` and `/auctions` map into this view, preserving queries and existing notification links. Market Trends and the Case Store remain contextual destinations; Collection prestige, Daily scores and friends live under Profile. Settlement APIs and feature flags retain their existing rules.

The Daily menu separates the featured Daily challenge, its J€/case/XP rewards and five-lot progress from Free Play and Higher or Lower. Signed-in Start/Continue/View results state comes from the saved account; guest progress and the labelled browser-history statistics remain local. The menu reuses the existing play actions and leaves all three game screens and rules unchanged.

Inventory cards show the image, title, rarity, per-item market estimate and availability. Clicking anywhere except the card’s action opens the item details dialog; images, valuation/acquisition details and store-management options live there, with secondary sections collapsed initially. Quick list publishes one available copy for 15 minutes at 70% of its displayed market estimate (minimum J€1), through the existing resale flow, and refreshes Inventory without discarding filters or shelved stock. Cases retain their Open case shortcut and can be listed from details. Custom quantity, price and duration use the existing auction form.

## Private accounting

`item_acquisitions` records the current owner's cost separately from tradable item JSON. `trader_sales` records immutable seller proceeds and cost inside the existing sale transaction, keyed by the source transaction and inventory ID. A paid transfer replaces the new owner's basis without changing the old seller's receipt. Failed transactions roll back balances, ownership and accounting together; existing transaction receipts keep retries safe.

One-item case openings inherit the current owner's case basis. Palettes and purchased stock bundles split the actual final paid price equally in persisted item order; early positions receive rounding remainders. Free Daily rewards have zero cost and their sale proceeds are shown separately. Legacy items have unknown cost and are excluded from realized profit. Opening an existing paid case is not a new reinvestment.

Private profiles expose available `tokens`, `heldInBids`, `inventoryMarketValue`, `realizedTradingProfit`, `rewardProceeds`, `unknownCostSales`, `traderStock` and persistent `traderMilestones`. Authenticated inventory adds `acquisitionCost`, `acquisitionSource`, `businessType` and `storeId`; `includeStores=1` opts into stocked inventory, preserving the old default. Public listings, storefronts, friends and rankings expose neither acquisition records nor proxy ceilings. Historical real-auction euro prices remain clearly separate from J€ market estimates.

Realized trading profit includes tracked paid item sales only. Shop investment, operating events and theft fines are separate from that measure. Revenue and markup over market estimate are never described as profit. Collection prestige continues to rank held collection value; the independent Daily leaderboard ranks completed Daily scores.

Inventory also shows private net worth with 1-day, 1-week, 30-day and all-time views. Total wealth subtracts outstanding fines and combines available J€, funds held in current winning proxy bids, every unsold owned item at its current market estimate, unopened won palettes at their public winning price, and owned stores at their current opening cost (including location). Listed finds and store stock count once through inventory. Sealed palette reward values remain hidden until reveal. These are estimated asset values, not sale proceeds.

`net_worth_snapshots` stores real observations from the start of tracking; there is no historical backfill. Profile activity retains the latest observation per minute, and the economy runtime samples human accounts at five-minute intervals, including offline players. The authenticated `/api/account/net-worth?range=1d|1w|1m|all` endpoint returns only the caller's history, bounded display points, and the current component totals. Range changes preserve inventory filters. Resetting the economy clears the old wealth history along with the old assets.

## Progress and risk

Complete Daily → manage a find → complete a sale → reinvest. Milestones are persistent, non-blocking, and award no additional currency. Guidance follows outstanding listings or store stock and offers trading alternatives when stock, funds or plots are unavailable. Purchasing a store shows price, remaining spending money and compatible owned stock; restocking links carry the store category into Auction House.

New random store mishaps are cosmetic. Existing event outcomes remain unchanged, and positive customer events keep their existing payouts. All other human-owned stores allow theft through free single-deck blackjack. Hit or stand; aces count as 1 or 11, face cards as 10, and the dealer stands on soft 17. A two-card natural beats another 21. No split, double, insurance or extra wager applies. Wins transfer the existing item with zero acquisition cost; pushes give neither theft nor fine. Deck order and the dealer's hole card stay server-private. Transactions and turn/request receipts prevent rerolls and duplicate transfers.

Losses enter a server-persisted police wheel with 80% escape and 20% caught. Escaping gives no loot and no fine. Wins and pushes skip the wheel. Only a caught wheel incurs the frozen fine: `max(20, ceil(liveMarketValue × 4 × guardFactor × max(1, 1/districtFactor)))`. A goose guard doubles the fine; security and location never alter card odds. Fines leave the economy rather than reward the store owner. Cash pays first, followed by inventory at current market estimates; seizure includes owned store stock and unbid listings, returns excess liquidation value, and does not count as a trading sale. Bidder-backed listings and unopened palette reward inventory remain protected until the existing ownership/reveal flows make assets available. Outstanding debt blocks further theft and is collected on account activity or the runtime. Net worth subtracts outstanding debt.

If no unsold inventory remains and the fine is still unpaid, the remainder becomes a jail sentence instead of perpetual debt: 5 minutes for the first recorded offence, 15 for the second, 45 for the third, then threefold increases capped at 24 hours. Offences count every catch. Jail persists across sessions and restarts and gates account/game APIs and signed-in economy routes; session state, sign-out and password access remain available. The app shows a blocking jail screen with a release countdown and checks server release automatically.

An unfinished hand becomes a loss after ten minutes, including offline and abandoned attempts. Its wheel gets two minutes to be spun and then resolves automatically, using the same saved roll. Closing, reloading and retries cannot cancel or reroll the wheel. A missing shelf item prevents a winning transfer without a fine. Actor/store cooldowns remain 30/5 minutes. Legacy paid attempts retain their frozen fees, rolls and ready times; the legacy opt-out field no longer restricts theft.
